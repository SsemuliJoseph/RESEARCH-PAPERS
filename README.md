## 📝 How I Read Research Papers

When reviewing a paper, I aim to understand more than just its abstract.

### 1. Problem

**What problem is the paper trying to solve?**

* What is the research question?
* Why is the problem important?
* What limitations exist in previous approaches?

### 2. Methodology

**How does the proposed approach work?**

* Model architecture
* Algorithms
* Dataset
* Training procedure
* Experimental setup
* Evaluation methodology

### 3. Results

**What did the researchers discover?**

* Main findings
* Performance metrics
* Comparisons with existing approaches
* Important experimental observations

### 4. Contributions

**What does the paper add to the field?**

I look for:

* New methods
* New datasets
* New benchmarks
* New findings
* Improvements over previous work
* Practical applications

### 5. Limitations

I also consider:

* Dataset limitations
* Computational requirements
* Generalization issues
* Bias
* Reproducibility
* Areas that remain unsolved

### 6. Research Opportunities

Finally:

> **What could be done next?**

This section is particularly useful for identifying potential projects, experiments, and future research directions.
